Studio Session

Building a Culture of Collaboration & Creativity · Pre-Service Educator Workshop

50-minute workshop · Groups of 4

Before you begin

This session is built around a simple idea: you'll learn collaboration and creativity techniques by using them, not just hearing about them. Each activity below is a technique you can take directly into your own classroom or practicum. Work through the four sessions in order — each one uses a timer, so keep an eye on the clock as you move.

⏱ 50 min total 👥 Groups of 4 🎯 4 activities

By the end of this session, you'll be able to:

  1. Apply a divergent-then-convergent creativity technique to a real classroom design problem.
  2. Practice two structured collaboration protocols (constraint-based jigsaw, gallery-walk critique) you can reuse with your own students.
  3. Give and receive peer feedback using a low-stakes, specific critique protocol.
  4. Draft one concrete way to bring collaborative-creative structures into your next lesson.

Facilitator Guide

Everything you need to run this session, start to finish.

Materials Checklist

  • Sticky notes — 3 colors (enough for 2 per participant in yellow/teal, plus 1 pad per group)
  • Chart paper or whiteboard space — 1 sheet/section per group
  • Markers — at least 1 per group
  • Printed constraint cards (Segment 2) — 1 per group, no repeats within a room
  • Printed teaching-scenario handout (Segment 2) — 1 per group
  • Exit ticket half-sheets (3-2-1 reflection) — 1 per participant
  • Room set up in movable pods of 4 before participants arrive

Timing Overview

SegmentTimeGroupingWhat you're watching for
1 · Spark — Rapid Ideation7 minSolo → pairsAre participants generating quantity without self-editing? Redirect any early judging.
2 · Build — Classroom Design Sprint20 minGroups of 4Is the constraint actually shaping their design, or being ignored? Prompt groups that stall.
3 · Share & Critique — Gallery Walk10 minWhole room, silentAre comments specific and kind? Model a strong sticky-note comment if the room is quiet.
4 · Reflect & Transfer8 minSoloLook for one genuinely specific, classroom-ready idea per participant — not a vague summary.

Facilitation Tips

  • Uneven groups: If a group of 4 isn't possible, a group of 3 can double up on one constraint role rather than dropping the protocol.
  • Quiet participants: In Segment 1's pair-share, name whose idea gets built on first so no one is left waiting to be picked.
  • Groups that stall in Segment 2: Give a nudge, not an answer — "What would this look like if you leaned harder into your constraint instead of working around it?"
  • Keeping the gallery walk silent: Say explicitly that talking happens after, not during — silence is what makes people write instead of just chatting.
  • Time pressure: If you're tight on time, Segment 3 can shrink to 6 minutes (1 comment per design instead of 2) before Segment 4.

Evaluation Rubric

CriterionEmergingProficientExemplary
Collaboration One or two voices dominate the group's design. Most group members contribute and build on each other's ideas. All members contribute; group visibly builds on and credits each other's ideas.
Creativity / Originality Design closely follows a familiar, conventional format. Design includes at least one clearly original element. Design reframes the problem in a genuinely unexpected way.
Use of Constraint Constraint is acknowledged but doesn't shape the final design. Constraint visibly shapes the design's key choices. The constraint becomes a creative asset the design is built around.
Feedback Quality Comments are general ("cool idea!") without specifics. Comments name a specific element and why it works or doesn't. Comments are specific, kind, and suggest a concrete next step.
